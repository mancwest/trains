The honest bit: each Lotto and Powerball draw is independent and random, using an independently audited machine with no memory. No formula, streak, or "overdue number" theory improves your odds — every combination has exactly the same chance, every time. The tools below are genuinely useful for exploring history, checking the draw looks statistically fair, and understanding what a ticket is actually worth — not for predicting outcomes.

Randomness audit

chi-square goodness-of-fit

Tests whether the loaded draws look statistically consistent with a fair, uniform random draw. This is real statistics, not a "hot numbers" gimmick — but small sample sizes mean it has limited power to detect anything even if it existed. Treat it as a sanity check, not proof of anything.

Wheeling system

guaranteed coverage, not odds

Pick a pool of numbers you like (6–20 of them). This generates a set of lines from that pool using a covering-design algorithm, so that if enough of the actual winning numbers land inside your pool, some of your lines are mathematically guaranteed to contain them together — not left to chance across your own tickets. This is the same family of maths Stefan Mandel used, just scaled down to something reasonable rather than millions of tickets. It does not change your odds of winning the jackpot — only how well your own multiple lines cover your own chosen numbers.
